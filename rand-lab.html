ℹ️ What Rand really does

Press Rand on your calculator and you get something like 0.639284 — always between 0 and 1, never the same twice.

Why 0 to 1? Because every other random thing can be built from it! 🔧

🎲 A die: Rand × 6, chop decimals, + 1
🪙 A coin: Rand < 0.5 → heads
🔢 A range: floor(Rand × (high−low+1)) + low

🔒 The big secret: computers and calculators can't actually be random. They follow a rule — a hidden starting number called a seed — and then pretend. That's why scientists call it pseudo-random: “sort of random”. 🎭

🎮 Why it matters: mathematicians use random numbers to solve real problems — shuffling cards, predicting weather, testing medicines, and even finding π (that's the darts game!). It has a grand name: Monte Carlo method, named after the casino city. 🎰
