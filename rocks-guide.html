⛰️ Granite vs Volcanic Tuff

Charlotte's Rock Detective Field Guide · the two rocks Hong Kong is made of

🪨 GRANITE — cooled underground

Slow-cooled magma, deep down. Like a tray of jelly left in the fridge overnight — the crystals had time to grow big. Grains: coarse, speckly, sugar-cookie texture.

🌋 TUFF — blasted out of a volcano

Erupted ash, welded solid. A volcano blasted out hot ash and pumice — then it all got squashed and stuck together. Grains: fine, smooth, matte — like a coffee mug.

🔍 The 20-second field test

  1. Wet it and look closely. Can you name the specks? → granite.
  2. Are the specks too small to name, and the rock smooth/matte? → probably tuff.
  3. Any flat, stretched, squashed blobs or streak-lines? → almost certainly tuff.
  4. Still stuck? Scratch it with a steel nail. Granite is stubborn; tuff often gives way.

🌋 Hong Kong's secret supervolcano

Around 140–164 million years ago, Hong Kong was a place of gigantic volcanic eruptions. Ash and hot pumice shot into the air, fell back, and welded into tuff. Below ground, the leftover magma cooled slowly into granite.

So Hong Kong is a two-layer city: tuff on top (from the eruptions) and granite below (the old magma chamber). That's why your school rock and Lion Rock are both granite — and why the rocks at Sai Kung are tuff!

And the most famous bit: in Sai Kung, the tuff cooled into enormous hexagonal columns — some of the biggest in the world, now a UNESCO Global Geopark. People call them “the Earth's honeycomb.”
