🎈 THE YEAST BREATH LAB

Blow up a balloon with living creatures · for Charlotte 🧙‍♀️

🎒 Go and gather these

  • A small plastic bottle (500 ml is perfect) — clean and empty 💧
  • Yeast — 1 teaspoon (borrow some from your mum!) 🥄
  • Sugar — 1 teaspoon 🍬
  • Warm water — half a cup. Warm-bath warm, never hot ☕
  • A balloon 🎈
  • A timer — that's the next tab! ⏱️
  • Optional: a ruler or string, to measure the balloon 📏
⚠️ Three important rules
1. Ask a grown-up first — and tell them yeast is alive, so this is real science! 🙋‍♀️
2. NEVER screw a lid on the bottle. The gas builds up pressure and the bottle can pop. The balloon is the lid. 🎈
3. Don't drink it. It's a science experiment, not a smoothie. 🙅‍♀️
💡 Ask your mum if she has fast-action / instant yeast — that works fastest. Plain dried yeast works too, it just takes a bit longer.

🔬 What's actually happening?

Those tiny living creatures are breathing — and the balloon is catching their breath. 💨

Yeast eats sugar and turns it into two things:

  • 💨 carbon dioxide gas → this fills the balloon
  • 🍺 alcohol → stays in the water (that's why it smells beery!)

Scientists have a fancy name for it: fermentation. 📚

🔥 Why warm water?
Yeast are little living creatures, so they work faster when they're cosy. Warm bath = fast. Cold = slow and sleepy. Hot = dead. That's why your mum checks the temperature — too hot and you've boiled your helpers alive. 🥵
🍞 This is EXACTLY how bread works.
In your bread dough there's no balloon — the gas gets trapped inside the stretchy dough instead. Same creatures, same burps, same gas. You're not doing a trick — you're doing what your mum's bread is doing right now. 💛

🔢 One more wonder: one yeast cell can make a baby every 90 minutes or so. Start with one at breakfast and by bedtime… you'd have hundreds. They never stop budding! 💦
